Key findings

  • Boston finished 64–18 with a +11.34-point scoring margin per game, leading the 2023–24 league.
  • That margin ranks second among the 330 team-seasons studied, behind the 2016–17 Warriors at +11.63.
  • Only four of the 11 champions led their league in regular-season scoring margin. Dominant regular-season results did not guarantee a title.

01 / THE QUESTION

The question

The Celtics won 64 regular-season games in 2023–24. Raw wins are awkward to compare across these champions because the 2019–20 Lakers played 71 games and the 2020–21 Bucks played 72. I compared win percentage instead: wins divided by games played.

03 / THE METHOD

The SQL method

I kept one row per championship team, with its season, wins, and losses. The query calculates the same measure shown above and sorts the results. The public sample ↗ runs this query against a five-row CSV in an in-memory SQLite database.

SELECT team_name, year,
       wins, losses,
       ROUND(
         100.0 * wins /
         (wins + losses), 1
       ) AS win_pct
FROM championship_teams
ORDER BY win_pct DESC,
         year DESC;

Season records: Celtics, Lakers, Nuggets, Warriors, and Bucks (NBA).

04 / THE TAKEAWAY

The takeaway

Boston had the highest regular-season win rate in this five-champion sample: 78.0%, compared with 73.2% for the next team, the 2020 Lakers. That is a descriptive result about these five seasons. The sample contains only champions, so it cannot establish what predicts a title or which team was strongest across eras.

A predictive study would need all team-seasons, including teams that did not win, and a held-out test period. Other measures such as scoring and assists can describe style, but they cannot fix that sampling problem.
