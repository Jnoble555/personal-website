A closer look at championship teams.

How dominant were the 2023–24 Boston Celtics? I used SQL and Tableau to compare Boston with the league, 11 champions, and 330 regular-season team-seasons from 2013–14 through 2023–24.

01 / THE QUESTION

Dominant in their season. Unusual across eleven.

Boston's 64–18 record tells one part of the story. I wanted to understand how far the 2023–24 Celtics separated from their league, how that season compared with Boston's earlier teams, and where it stood among recent champions and non-champions.

The dashboard connects four views: a league-wide comparison of three-point attempt share and scoring margin, Boston's eleven-season margin trend, the eleven champions, and the ten highest-margin team-seasons in the study.

02 / THE DATASET

330 team-seasons, including non-champions.

The dataset covers all 30 NBA teams in each regular season from 2013–14 through 2023–24. Each of its 330 rows represents one team in one season; eleven rows are championship teams. Playoff games are excluded from the metrics, and championship outcomes are joined separately as labels.

Historical game records come from nbadb/Kaggle through 2022–23, with BoxScore Lab supplying 2023–24. Python normalizes team abbreviations and converts games into reciprocal team-game records, then SQLite aggregates the season totals. Tableau uses the derived season summaries.

The saved audit checks season coverage, expected game counts, reciprocal scores, and one champion per season. All 60 team records and scoring averages checked against BoxScore Lab standings for 2022–23 and 2023–24 reconcile without mismatches.

03 / THE METHOD

Aggregate first, then compare.

Scoring margin is total points scored minus total points allowed, divided by actual games played. It is not possession-adjusted net rating. Using actual games accounts for the shortened 2019–20 and 2020–21 seasons. Shooting and ball-management ratios divide season totals rather than averaging game-level percentages.

SQL window functions rank teams within each season and across the full study period. The historical ranking keeps non-champions in the comparison, so the dashboard can show that a strong regular season did not guarantee a title. This excerpt from the project's historical-margin view ranks the unrounded values:

SELECT season, team, games,
       wins, losses, margin_pg,
       is_champion,
       RANK() OVER (
         ORDER BY margin_pg DESC
       ) AS historical_margin_rank
FROM team_season;

The league scatterplot uses three-point attempt share: three-point attempts divided by all field-goal attempts. That measure describes shot selection, not shot quality or a causal explanation of winning. The champion panel's rank labels refer to each champion's scoring-margin rank within its own season.

04 / THE TAKEAWAY

Boston's separation was exceptional.

Boston led the 2023–24 league with a +11.34-point margin per game. Oklahoma City followed at +7.41, a gap of 3.93 points. Across all 330 team-seasons, Boston ranked second behind the 2016–17 Warriors at +11.63.

Boston's margin rose from +6.52 in 2022–23 to +11.34 in 2023–24. Its three-point attempt share fell slightly, from 47.98% to 47.08%, so the improvement cannot be explained simply as taking a larger share of threes.

Only four of the eleven champions led their regular-season league in scoring margin, although all eleven ranked in the top six. The 2015–16 Warriors and Spurs both exceeded a ten-point margin and did not win the title. The result supports a descriptive claim about Boston's dominance, not a rule for predicting champions.

Scope and limitations: this study covers 2013–14 through 2023–24, not all NBA history. Per-game margin is not adjusted for pace, schedule, overtime, or era. The analysis does not account for injuries, roster availability, or playoff opposition. Repeated franchises are not independent experiments, and no held-out prediction test or causal analysis was performed.
