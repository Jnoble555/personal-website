CASE STUDY 01 · ACTIVE PROJECT

From 50 miles to 100.

How can a personal training tool help me learn from one ultramarathon build while preparing for the next? UltraTrain starts with a factual baseline and follows the 28-week build toward the 2027 Umstead 100.

The question

My first 50-mile race gave me a result, but little structure for explaining what happened or deciding what to watch as I train for twice the distance. I wanted a tool that puts the completed training, race pacing, and upcoming plan in one place without presenting uncertain predictions as facts.

The evidence

I reconstructed the 2025 training block from my account export and separated training from race day. The longest training run was much shorter than the event, which is an interesting fact about the build, not a rule for planning the next race.

The export also includes a detailed race track. Its timing and distance closely agree with the activity summary, so I can show approximate elapsed pacing by 10-mile section. No heart-rate samples or consistent effort notes are available; pace cannot tell the full story of fatigue or conditions.

The tool

The first version generates a private dashboard from an account export. It shows weekly running volume, a 50-mile pacing breakdown, the 28-week Umstead plan, and planned versus recorded mileage. It also catches ambiguous date matches, such as a run recorded shortly after midnight.

The training dashboard is private. The public case study uses aggregate figures and excludes route coordinates, activity IDs, and personal notes.

What comes next

The project will grow alongside the actual training: short notes on effort, fueling, and recovery can explain details that GPS data misses. After the race, I can compare what I expected at each stage with what actually happened. A separate study of historical race splits may support a race-pacing model, with its own data source and evaluation.
